## US — most screens are value traps right now

The US "cheap + high FCF" universe in Sept 2026 is dominated by oil/gas drillers
(SM, NOG, CIVI, CHRD, GPOR, TALO) and structurally declining businesses (DXC).
A 30–60% FCF yield on a driller means the market expects oil to fall — not a gem.

| **CALM** — Cal-Maine Foods (eggs) | $3.7B | ROIC 24.7%, op margin 36%, P/E 5.4x | Promising *(est.)* | Earnings pumped by avian-flu egg-price spike; normalises down hard |
| **BBWI** — Bath & Body Works | $3.8B | ROIC 34%, op margin 15%, P/E 5.9x | Promising *(est.)* | Mall retail; well-covered, not a "blindspot" |
| **GPOR** — Gulfport Energy | $3.7B | ROIC 18%, op margin 42%, P/E 8.7x | Watch | Pure natural-gas price bet, no moat |
| **SM** — SM Energy | $3.3B | ROIC 16%, P/E 5.0x, FCF yield 62% | Watch | Oil cyclical; extreme FCF yield = market disbelief |
| **DXC** — DXC Technology | $2.2B | P/E 5.2x, FCF yield 52%, ROIC 11.7% | Likely trap | Revenue shrinks every year; cheap and getting cheaper |

**US verdict:** no clean multi-year "buy and forget" gem surfaced. If you want US
single-name exposure, CALM and BBWI are the only two worth a verification pass —
and both have a specific "why it's cheap" you'd be betting against.

## Do this before buying anything

Run these filters on a **real** screener (screener.in for India, Finviz/ChartMill
for US) — not off listicles:

**India (screener.in query):**
```
Market Capitalization > 500 AND Market Capitalization < 15000
AND Return on capital employed > 18
AND Debt to equity < 0.5
AND Profit growth 3Years > 15
AND Sales growth 3Years > 12
AND Promoter holding > 50
AND Price to Earning < Industry PE
```

**US (Finviz):**
```
Market cap: $300M–$10B
P/E < 15, PEG < 1.2, P/FCF < 15
ROIC > 12%, Operating margin > 12%
Debt/Equity < 0.6
EPS growth past 5y > 10%, EPS growth next 5y > 8%
Analyst count: < 6 (the "blindspot")
Insider transactions: positive
```

Then for each survivor, check by hand:
1. **Why is it cheap?** If you can't name the reason, you've missed it.
2. Is the recent profit growth **repeatable**, or a one-off (commodity spike,
   land sale, tax reversal)?
3. Revenue concentration — one customer / one product / one geography?
4. Promoter/insider behaviour — buying or selling?
5. Would you be fine holding it through a 50% drawdown for 3 years? If not, skip.

## Sizing rule

- Explorer sleeve = **5–10% of total**, split across **4–6 names minimum**.
- No single explorer name > 2% of total portfolio.
- Same monthly-SIP discipline; review holdings once a quarter, not daily.
- If the sleeve underperforms the core index for 3 years, shut it down — that's
  the pre-agreed exit, decided now while it's unemotional.
